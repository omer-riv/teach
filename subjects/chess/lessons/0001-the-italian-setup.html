Chess Openings · Lesson 1 · ~8 min

The Italian setup

Six White moves that give you a safe king and a good position against almost anything your friends play.

Why this first. In casual games, most early losses come from a king left in the middle or pieces that never come out. This setup takes care of both. You learn one plan instead of memorising lines.

Reading the moves

Every square has a name: a letter for the column (a–h, left to right from White's side) and a number for the row (1–8). The board below shows them along its edges. A move is written as the piece letter plus the square it lands on: N knight, B bishop, R rook, Q queen, K king. Pawns get no letter. Nf3 means "knight to f3", e4 means "pawn to e4", and O-O means castling on the king's side4.

Still finding squares slowly? The notation page has a quick tap-the-square drill. A minute a day makes it automatic.

The three jobs of an opening

  1. Take the centre. Pieces in the middle reach more squares.
  2. Develop your knights and bishops: move them off the back row, where they do nothing.
  3. Castle early, so your king hides behind pawns3.

Each of the six moves does one of these jobs. Step through them:

  1. A pawn takes the centre, and the diagonals open for your bishop and queen. Job 1.
  2. Black takes the centre too. This is the most common reply.
  3. Your knight comes out towards the centre and attacks the e5 pawn. Job 2.
  4. Black defends e5 with a knight.
  5. Your bishop aims at f7: the weak square next to Black's king, defended only by the king. This is the Italian Game1. Job 2.
  6. Black copies you. This is the usual reply, called the Giuoco Piano1.
  7. A quiet pawn move. It prepares d4 later, and gives your bishop a hiding square on c2 if it's ever chased.
  8. Black's knight now attacks your e4 pawn. A knight jumps from f6 straight to e4.
  9. Defend it. Now if the knight takes on e4, your pawn takes back.
  10. Black defends e5 the same way.
  11. Castle: the king goes two squares right and the rook jumps over it. King safe, rook in the game. Job 3.
  12. Black castles too. Both sides are ready for the middle game. That's the whole setup.

This calm version, with c3 and d3, is called the Giuoco Pianissimo ("very quiet game"): White keeps the centre closed and plays slowly1,2. The Italian Game is a popular recommendation for beginners2. Famous sharper lines exist (the Fried Liver, the Evans Gambit). They're fun, but they need memorising, and you don't need them to win casual games.

The one rule about order

The first three moves always go e4, Nf3, Bc4. After that, c3, d3 and O-O can come in any order, with one exception:

Black knight on f6? Play d3 first.

The knight on f6 attacks your e4 pawn. If Black plays it on move 3 (that's the Two Knights Defence2), defend at once with d3 and carry on.

Practice: play it from memory

You're White. Tap a piece, then the square to move it to. To castle, tap your king, then g1. Black's replies change from game to game, so play several games and watch for the knight on f6.

Aim for three games in a row with no mistakes. If you slip, that's useful: it's what makes the moves stick.

Check yourself

1. What does Bc4 aim at?

f7 sits next to Black's king and only the king defends it, so the bishop puts early pressure there.

2. After 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4, Black plays Nf6. Your move?

The knight on f6 attacks e4, so defend it with d3 first. Then c3 and castling.

3. Which pieces should you bring out first?

Knights and bishops are developed first. The queen comes out early only to get chased around.

4. Why castle early?

Castling puts the king behind a wall of pawns and brings a rook towards the centre.

5. What does the quiet move c3 prepare?

c3 supports a later d4. It also gives the bishop a retreat on c2. (It does take the c3 square from your knight.)

Your win: you can play the Italian setup as White from memory, and you know the one thing that changes the order.

Try it for real: play your next three games as White with this setup, against friends or a bot on your app. Don't worry about what comes after move 6 yet.
