Reading chess notation

How to read a move like Nf3 or Qxf7#, with pictures.

1. Every square has a name

A letter for the column (a to h), then a number for the row (1 to 8). From White's side, a is on the left and row 1 is nearest you1.

From Black's side everything turns around: h is on the left and row 8 is nearest you. The squares keep their names.

Practice: find the square

Tap the square named. Try it from both sides. A streak of 10 means you've got it.

2. A move is a piece plus a square

LetterPiece
KKing
QQueen
RRook
BBishop
NKnight (K is taken by the king)
nonePawn

The square is where the piece lands. You don't write where it came from.

4. Move numbers

Each number covers one White move and one Black move: 1. e4 e5 means White played e4, then Black played e5. When a line starts with Black's move, it uses three dots: 3… Nf6 means "Black's 3rd move, knight to f6".
