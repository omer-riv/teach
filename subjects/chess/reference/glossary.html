Glossary

The words this course uses, and what they mean here.

Algebraic notation
How moves are written: piece letter (K, Q, R, B, N; none for a pawn) plus the square it lands on. Nf3 = knight to f3. Squares are a–h (columns) and 1–8 (rows) from White's side.
Castling (O-O)
The king moves two squares towards a rook, and the rook jumps over it. O-O is the king's side (short); O-O-O the queen's side. Some apps write 0-0.
Centre
The four middle squares, e4, d4, e5 and d5. Pieces there reach the most squares.
Develop
Move a knight or bishop off the back row to a useful square.
Giuoco Piano
The Italian Game with 3… Bc5. Italian for "quiet game".
Giuoco Pianissimo
The Giuoco Piano played slowly with c3 and d3: the setup this course uses.
Italian Game
The opening 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4.
Opening
The first moves of a game, roughly until both sides have developed and castled.
Two Knights Defence
The Italian with 3… Nf6. The knight attacks e4, so White plays d3.
